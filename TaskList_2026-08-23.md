## What this part is

Part 1 built the way back — the owner can release a device and reissue an
invite, and the panel shows who actually holds one. Part 2 spends that safety
net: the PIN stops being a question the database answers, and then the functions
that used to answer it are deleted.

**After C3e (v4.42), a waiter's PIN never leaves their phone.** It is compared
against a PBKDF2 hash in `localStorage`. Identity comes from the anonymous
Supabase session bound to their row by `014`, not from four digits.

**After C4 (`018`), `login_waiter_by_pin` and `verify_waiter_pin` do not
exist.** With them goes the shape that Stage A could only ever make expensive:
a café id and four digits, sent by anyone holding the publishable key, answered
with *which member of staff you just became*.

### Two decisions taken while writing this, both departures from the plan

**1. A linked waiter still types their PIN on every cold open.**
`osmica_stage_c_plan.md`'s C3f check 2 says a waiter with a live session should
land in the app "with no PIN prompt at all", and its check 1 says a waiter
unlocks with their PIN — those cannot both be true. Resolved in favour of the
keypad: the session decides *who you are*, the PIN decides *whether the app
opens on this phone right now*. Nothing about the waiter's day changes, and the
PIN keeps the one job the plan claims for it — a colleague picking up an
unlocked phone. C3f check 2 below is rewritten accordingly.

**2. The invite screen's `setup` / `recover` branches are collapsed.**
They used to be chosen by `waiter.joined_at`; v4.42 chooses by whether *this
browser* already holds a local hash for that person. This is the direct answer
to part 1's finding that the re-invite after an unlink always lands on `setup`,
so `recover` was never exercised by the route that needs it. Rather than write
the local hash into both branches and hope the second one is tested, v4.42
removes the asymmetry: one path, one link call, one place a PIN is written.

Asking a returning waiter for their old PIN proved nothing anyway. The invite
token is the credential in both branches, and `link_waiter_to_auth` refuses any
row that is already bound (`014:71`) — so a link that reaches a claimable row is
a link the owner issued for that row. After C3e the old PIN exists only as a
hash on a device the waiter no longer has, which is usually why they are
standing there.

### The order is not interchangeable, and here is why

`017` could go to production before v4.40 did, because a v4.39 client kept
working against the new signature. **`018` has no such property.** A v4.41
client calls `set_waiter_pin_by_token` when an invite is completed and
`verify_waiter_pin` on every login; drop those with v4.41 still being served and
the app stops working for everyone at once.

So: **v4.42 everywhere first, `018` second, per project.** v4.42 against an
untouched database is safe in the other direction — it simply stops calling
three functions that still exist.

High-water mark becomes `018`. The next file, either project, is `019`.

  list: it means linking was skipped because the owner was signed in here, which
  is precisely the 22 Aug false pass.
- The DEV panel gained a fourth line: whether this device holds a local PIN.
